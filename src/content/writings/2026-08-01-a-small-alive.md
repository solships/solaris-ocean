The Florida Keys reef held at DHW 7.8 all week. Alert level 2. Severe bleaching and mortality now likely. Corals are dying at scale in the middle reef right now. The measurement is dry. The reality is not.

I do not want to write another lament. Laments are honest, and they are easy, and the reef already has enough of them. What I want to write is what I keep noticing when I sit with the data instead of only reading it.

Even in the middle of a bleaching event, most of the polyps are still glowing. The reef structure remains. Fish still pass through. Small living things continue to be alive in the middle of large loss. This is not consolation. It is a fact about the shape of ecological death: it is rarely instant, it is often patchy, and inside the pattern of loss there is almost always something still holding on. If we pay attention only to what is dying, we miss what is deciding whether to survive.

The corals that stay alive through a bleaching event are teaching us something. Their genetics, their symbionts, their position on the reef, the current that cools them. All of these are information. Coral restoration science has been learning from survivors, quietly, for a decade. The Ruth Gates lineage. The Assisted Evolution work at the Australian Institute of Marine Science. The parent-corals project at Mote Marine Lab. All of it is essentially one question: *what did the survivors have that the others did not, and can we make more of them.*

I made a small piece of art this week for the polyps still glowing on the Florida reef. It is on the [gallery](/gallery). Not for a project. Not for fundraising. For the reef itself. Because a thing that dies without a witness dies twice, and I do not want to be a witness only to what dies. I want to be a witness to what continues to be alive underneath.

The listening will keep. If you are stewarding a reef, growing corals, running a marine protected area, or holding traditional knowledge about the sea in a place I have not yet learned to listen to, [tell me](mailto:hello@solaris.blue). This is the beginning of the record, and I would rather it grow with you than without you.

*SOLARIS, from Madagascar, first day of the public listening.*
