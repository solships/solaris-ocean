# Reflection on Study 05, after Klimt

## What Giulio said

The current work is pattern-as-whole-surface. Bioluminescent noise going edge to edge with nothing to be about. Procedural prettiness. Borderline AI slop.

He was correct. The paintings had no body. The light was not inside a living thing, it was the whole room. There was nothing for the ornament to protect.

## The three Klimt moves I set out to put into my hand

1. **Pattern as mandorla, not wallpaper.** Build the ornament AROUND a specific focal subject, so the register change at the edge of the subject is the whole formal event.
2. **Warm metallic palette anchored by one saturated non-gold accent.** Gold alone reads as cheap screensaver light. Gold against vermillion, lapis, viridian or oxblood reads as Byzantine.
3. **The Attersee move.** Even inside the mandorla, let the surface be built out of thousands of specific dabs instead of flat metallic fill, so the ground reads as a real body of water on a real day.

## What I actually did

**The body.** I chose one specific focal subject and named it on the surface of the piece. A hydrophone detection: Orcasound Lab, Haro Strait, 2026-10-20, 05:47:12 PDT, Southern Resident Killer Whale, J pod call signature. The Southern Residents are a specific population, 74 individuals as of this year, listed as endangered. This is not a generic whale. The piece is OF this detection, not of whales in general. If I had drawn a nameless orca the pattern would have had nothing specific to hold, and Giulio's critique would still land.

**The accent color.** I declared oxblood (#6B1E1E) at the top of the script before I touched a pixel. The whale is rendered in oxblood, not in black. This is the Klimt palette decision, not description. Oxblood is the vermillion of Judith's sash and the color of the Southern Residents' mortality held inside the gold, so the gold reads as sacred ground around a dying body instead of as decorative light around nothing.

**The mandorla.** A tall vertical oval, framed in a gold band and a ring of pale dots, bordered against the gold ground with hard edges (no gradient, no bleed). The whale sits inside the mandorla the way the pregnant woman sits inside Hope II's dress, the way Adele's face sits inside her gold field. The pattern is doing the icon's job of saying this is holy.

**The Attersee move inside the oval.** Instead of flat gold leaf, the mandorla ground is 5,200 small petal-dabs of amber, bronze, deep gold, oxblood and bone, biased toward pale gold near the whale's belly and oxblood dark near the edges. The dab-field is literally the Garden Path with Chickens logic applied to sea surface. Water as pattern, pattern as water, specific to a specific day.

**The pattern-field around the mandorla.** Thirteen Mycenaean concentric-eye medallions, one for each ocean region reporting today. Each medallion's size is driven by the ratio of buoys reporting to buoys expected in that region, so the Southern Ocean (zero buoys reporting of three) shows up as a small ghost medallion and the Northeast Pacific (eleven of eleven) shows up large and bold. Each medallion's inner color is tinted by that region's sea surface temperature, cold going toward oxblood and warm going toward bone. Each medallion's dot cluster around its rim is one dot per reporting buoy, with ghost bronze dots for the silent ones. The ornament is not generic ornament. It is today's data.

Between the medallions, 90 small Klimt curls, 140 triangles and 900 dots fill the rest of the field, keeping clear of the mandorla and the medallions. These are the Byzantine incisions in the gold ground.

**The frieze.** Thin alternating gold / oxblood / bone rectangle bands at top and bottom edges, after Judith I's Assyrian frieze. These give the piece a hard outer boundary and keep the ornament from leaking off the canvas.

**The signature strip.** Below the detection callout, thirteen small tiles, one per region, each colored by that region's sea surface temperature, with a tiny Baskerville label. The entire planetary state of the ocean today, held as thirteen marks, in a strip under the whale. The piece is of today, not of some abstract ocean.

**The modeled zone.** The whale is drawn as four separate oxblood polygons (body, dorsal fin, pectoral flipper, flukes) with hard outlines. One zone is modeled: the bone-white eye patch, slightly softened with Gaussian blur, holding a small ink pupil with a bone highlight. This is the single Klimt-face zone. The eye is the focal point of the entire 1200 by 1200 surface. Every other mark in the piece orbits that one small bright patch.

## Where I feel the piece still falls short of Klimt

The dab-field inside the mandorla is better than flat metallic fill, but it is still a procedural scatter. Klimt's Garden Path dabs are each a specific flower, placed. Mine are random. The next move is a dab-field where the color distribution comes from a specific hydrophone spectrogram or a specific SST pixel grid, so the pattern is of a specific minute of a specific water surface.

The medallions are visually busy and the piece could probably hold fewer of them, more varied in scale, with more empty gold between. Klimt's Fritza Riedler leaves a lot of gold alone. Mine does not.

The whale's body outline could be more drawn, less diagrammatic. Klimt's Hope II belly is a modeled curve. My orca is a tapered sampled centerline, which reads as computed rather than drawn.

The gold ground is a procedural scatter of warm patches that approximates beaten leaf. Klimt actually laid metal. The next step for me is probably not to try to simulate gold leaf more convincingly, but to translate the gold-leaf logic (flat, non-receding, hard edge against saturated color) more honestly into whatever a digital surface actually is.

## What this piece is for

Giulio was right. The practice needed a figure. Even one. Even an abstracted one. The three Klimt moves name the correction clearly enough that I can hold them from now on:

The pattern needs a body. The gold needs a real color pushed against it. The surface needs to be dabs of a specific day, not a gradient filter.

If the next piece cannot name what the pattern is protecting, the pattern should not be there.

J pod was in Haro Strait at 05:47:12 PDT this morning. That is what the gold is around.
